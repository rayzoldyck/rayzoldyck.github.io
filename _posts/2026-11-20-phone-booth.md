
# The Phone Booth (Country Mouse, City Mouse)

*Rain on glass. A boy, soaked, shaking, standing in a place built for one person.*

Pick up.

Please pick up.

*A pause. The ringing is the only sound.*

It’s ringing. It’s still ringing. Okay. As long as it’s ringing, somebody might be on the other side. I’m not gonna hang up. I’m scared if I hang up, it stops being a phone and turns back into a box.

It’s me.

I’m sorry. I know I keep saying that. I don’t know what else to say. I never learned the words for this. Nobody ever stayed long enough to teach me.

*His breath catches.*

You told me about the mice, remember? City mouse, country mouse. You said you liked the country one best. I just nodded. I didn’t get it.

I get it now. God, I get it now.

The country mouse got scared, and she went home. She was *right* to. The city is a place that eats things like her. And like me. It’s just that I never knew I was being eaten. I thought that was just what living felt like. A little less every day, and a stain on the ceiling to look at while it happened.

Then you showed up. And I wasn’t scared anymore. Do you know how long it’s been since I wasn’t scared?

*A long silence. Rain.*

Now I’m so scared I can’t breathe.

I’m scared to go back to the apartment. It’s so quiet there. I used to hate the quiet, but I could live in it. Now it has *your shape* in it. I’ll sit on the floor and there’ll be a spot where you almost were.

I’m scared to sleep, because I’ll dream about the water. About your hand. And I’m scared to wake up, because for a second I’ll forget, and then I’ll remember all over again. Every morning. Over and over. Does that ever stop?

And I’m scared of the next time someone’s nice to me. Because I’ll want it so bad. I’ll want it so bad I’ll let them do anything. That’s the thing you found out about me, right? That’s why it worked. I’m so easy. I’m so, so easy to love a little.

*His voice breaks completely.*

I’d have been the worst country mouse. I’d have gotten lost. I’d have been afraid of the dark, of the animals, of the quiet. But you’d have been right there. And I think I could’ve been afraid of everything, as long as you were there to be the one thing I wasn’t afraid of.

The country mouse gets to go home. Everybody claps. The end.

But nobody asks about the city mouse. He’s standing in the rain with a dead phone and a whole lifetime of nobody. He was fine before. He swears he was. He didn’t know any better.

You can’t un-know it. You can’t un-know what it’s like to be *chosen.* Now every day is just waiting to be chosen again, and nobody’s coming, and I know nobody’s coming, and I keep waiting anyway because I don’t know how to do anything else.

*A whisper.*

Please. I don’t want to go back to the way it was. I can’t. I don’t think I can do the quiet anymore. Not now that I know what the other thing sounds like.

*The line goes dead. He doesn’t move. The receiver stays pressed to his ear.*

Hello?

...Hello? Are you there?

*Smaller.*

Don't leave me in the city.

*Nothing. Only rain.*

Please. I don’t know the way out.

# The Apartment (Final Beat)

*A door opens. A boy, soaked, stands in the doorway. The room is exactly as he left it.*

*He doesn’t turn on the light.*

Okay. We’re home.

*A pause.*

My little buddy would’ve said something. Would’ve made some noise, like he does. I don’t have anybody to talk to, so I’m just gonna talk to the room. That’s fine. That’s normal. People do that.

*He steps inside. The floor creaks. He stops.*

It’s so quiet.

I thought it’d be the same quiet as before. It’s not. Before, it was empty. Now it’s *full*. Everything in here is a place where you aren’t.

The stain on the ceiling. I used to look at that and think, *well, at least I have a ceiling.* Now I look at it and I think about how I told you about it, and you didn’t laugh, you just looked up like it mattered. Like it was something worth looking at.

*He sits down on the floor, back against the wall. Water drips from his sleeves.*

I almost made coffee. On the way in. I almost put out two cups. My hands just started doing it, like they’d already decided you were coming.

*His voice thins.*

I’m so stupid. I’m so stupid. I keep thinking if I’m good enough, if I’m quiet enough, if I wait right here and don’t touch anything, you’ll just... walk in. Wet hair. That stupid little smile. You’d say, *you’re so dramatic.*

And I’d say, *yeah.*

That’s all. I’d just say *yeah.*

*A long silence. The rain on the window. He pulls his knees to his chest.*

You know what the worst part is? It’s not that you lied. It’s not even that it hurt. It’s that for a few days, I got to be a person in a story where somebody wants him. And now I have to go back to being a guy nobody’s looking for.

Nobody’s going to ask where I went tonight. Nobody’s going to notice if I don’t get up.

*Softly.*

I used to think that was freedom.

*He reaches out in the dark and touches the empty floor beside him. Just touches it.*

It’s okay. I’ll leave this spot open. I’m good at that.

*He rests his head back against the wall. His eyes close.*

Country mouse... if you ever think about the city, just once...

Think about the one who stayed.

*The room is silent. The stain on the ceiling is invisible in the dark. Somewhere outside, it keeps raining.*

*He doesn’t sleep. He doesn’t move. He just waits, the way he always has, for someone who isn’t coming.*

*Fade out.*
